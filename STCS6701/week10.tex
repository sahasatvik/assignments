\begin{flushleft}
\textbf{%
    The validity of posterior expansions based on Laplace’s method (Geisser et al., 1990)
} \\
Satvik Saha (ss7228) \\
\today
\end{flushleft}

\vspace{0.1in}


Laplace's method is a classical tool for approximating high-frequency integrals \[
    \int_\R g(x)\, e^{-n h(x)}\:dx
        = \sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{nh''(x_0)}}\,e^{-nh(x_0)}\left(g(x_0) + \mathcal{O}\left(n^{-1}\right)\right)
\] as $n \to \infty$, where $x_0$ is the unique global minimizer of $h$.
The key idea is to approximate \[
    h(x_0) + \frac{1}{2}(h''(x_0) - \epsilon)(x - x_0)^2
        \leq h(x)
        \leq h(x_0) + \frac{1}{2}(h''(x_0) + \epsilon)(x - x_0)^2
\] for sufficiently small $0 < \epsilon < h''(x_0)$ around a
$\delta$-neighborhood of $x_0$.
This essentially turns the integrand of interest into an unnormalized Gaussian
density, with nearly all of its mass concentrated in a vanishingly small
neighborhood of $x_0$!

In Bayesian inference, the posterior density of $\theta$ under some prior
$\pi(\theta)$ and likelihood $L(y\mid \theta)$ is described by \[
    \E[g(\theta) \mid y] = \frac{\int g(\theta)\, L(y\mid \theta)\pi(\theta) \:d\theta}{\int L(y\mid \theta)\pi(\theta)\:d\theta},
\] which can be approximated using Laplace's method, expanding around the MAP
$\hat{\theta}$.
The main difference between this and the classical setup is that the
log-likelihood function $h$ (hence its maximizer $x_0$ too) depends on the data
$y_1, \dots, y_n$.
\cite{geisser1990} formalizes conditions under which this kind of approximation
does indeed work; call this \emph{Laplace regularity} of log-likelihoods.
One (very neat!) consequence is that for nice enough likelihoods, we have
posterior concentration inequalities of the form \[
    \Pr(|\theta - \hat{\theta}| \geq \delta \mid y) < e^{-nc}.
\]
